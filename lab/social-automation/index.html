Social and chat automation

A sandbox for drafting social posts and answering routine chat messages for a small business, with a human approving every post and rules that stop the bot inventing prices.

01

The situation

A small business in Windhoek needs steady social posts and quick WhatsApp-style answers, without hiring someone for it, but it cannot afford a bot that invents prices, discounts or promises. This lab shows the shape of a safe version: automation drafts the post and handles the routine questions, rules block the risky wording, and a person approves or takes over. The business, the offers and the conversations below are fictional samples.

02

What to look at

  1. Generate a week of drafts, then type a price or a "50% off" into a draft and watch the rules block it before it can be approved.
  2. Chat with the bot: ask for opening hours, ask what a cake costs (it refuses to guess), then book a table with a bad party size to see validation and hand-off.
  3. Open the test table at the bottom: every row is a real message run through the same engine, with a live pass or fail.

      How it is built

      One vanilla JavaScript file (labs/social-automation.js), no libraries and no network calls. The calendar is a template composer plus a rule checker; the bot is a small state machine, step(state, text), that returns the replies, the nodes it fired and the trace lines. The flow graph, the transcript, the trace and the test table all read from that one function, so they cannot disagree.

      • Rules, not guesses. The checker flags numbers and discount words that are not in the brief you typed, absolute claims such as "best" or "guaranteed", a missing call to action and posts over the channel limit. Wording that could mislead is a blocker: the Approve button stays disabled until it is fixed.
      • A person decides. Every post waits in a queue to be approved, edited, rejected or regenerated; only approved posts can be exported.
      • A bot with edges. Prices are never quoted, an instruction to ignore the rules is ignored, two misses or three failed answers in a booking hand the conversation to a person.
      • Where AI would go. A language-model step could draft the wording or the reply, but it would sit behind these same checks and the same hand-off, and it would be added on the server so no key reaches the browser. That is deliberately not connected here.

      05

      Limits and sources

      • The business, the offers, the opening hours, the names and every conversation are fictional samples. No real business, price or person is involved.
      • The drafts come from templates, not from an AI. The bot is a keyword rule set, not natural-language understanding, so it will miss phrasings a real flow would learn.
      • Nothing is posted, sent or saved. Reloading the page clears everything; the downloads are built in your browser.
      • This mirrors only what the main site already says about the chatbot platform (a flow engine with conditions, user-input steps, AI reply steps and hand-off to a human); it is not that platform and does not show its scale.
