Lab · E-commerce

Shop checkout and images

Real photographs, loading measured in your own browser, and a complete test purchase from product page to confirmation.

01

The situation

A small shop's photographs are usually the heaviest part of its page. On a phone with a weak signal they arrive slowly, and when they arrive without reserved space the text and buttons jump under the visitor's thumb. The checkout is the next place a buyer gives up: a long form, vague error messages, a fee that appears only at the end. This lab takes a made-up shop that sells things you could find at a Namibian market (baskets, clay bowls, cloth dolls, wool, carvings, dried fruit and fish) and shows both problems being handled.

02

What to look at

  1. Run the comparison. The same eight photographs are placed twice: once the careless way, once the careful way. The numbers (layout shift, when the largest image was painted, bytes) are measured by your own browser as the images load.
  2. Read the connection table. It works out how long each version would take at four assumed connection speeds, from the real file sizes.
  3. Buy something. Open a product, pick a size, change the quantity in the cart, choose a delivery region, then try to place the order with the form empty to see the errors. All of it is test data.

A. Loading: careless markup against careful markup

The two grids below are built one after the other and measured separately; the page scrolls each into view first, because a browser only counts what moves on screen. Your connection is probably faster than a customer's on mobile data, so also try the simulated delay: it holds the images back for the same time in both grids so the jumping becomes visible. It is a simulation, not your network.

04

How it is built

  • Photographs. Real photos from Wikimedia Commons, accepted only when the page reports CC0, public domain, CC BY or CC BY-SA. scripts/fetch-photos.mjs checks that before it saves anything, writes the author and licence into scripts/photos.json, and rejects anything else. Originals stay in an ignored folder. scripts/build-lab-shop-images.py crops each to 4:3 and writes WebP and JPEG at 400, 800 and 1200 px, plus a 16 px blurred placeholder.
  • Careful markup. Fixed width and height plus a CSS aspect ratio so the box exists before the file arrives; srcset and sizes so a phone takes the 400 px file; WebP with a JPEG fallback inside picture; loading="lazy" below the first row; decoding="async"; a tiny blurred placeholder behind each photo until it arrives.
  • Measuring. A PerformanceObserver watches layout-shift and largest-contentful-paint entries; the image load events and Resource Timing give load times and bytes. Each grid is built and settled on its own so the numbers do not mix, and every image URL carries a one-off query string so the browser cannot answer from cache.
  • Checkout. Plain DOM code, no framework. Prices include VAT, as shop prices usually do, so the summary shows the VAT contained in the total. Each field has a label and a hint; on submit the errors are collected into a summary that takes focus and links to each field, and every field also shows its own message tied to it with aria-describedby. Cart changes are announced in a polite live region.
  • Trade-offs. The cart is in memory only, so a reload clears it; that keeps the page free of cookies and storage. The slow-connection table is arithmetic, not a network test. Layout Instability and Largest Contentful Paint are Chromium features, so other browsers show “not supported” for those rows.

05

Limits and sources

  • Sample, not real. The shop, products, prices, sizes, delivery zones and fees are invented for this demo. There are no customers, orders or sales behind it. The “order” is never stored or sent, and no payment is taken.
  • VAT. The sample uses a flat rate of 15%, the standard VAT rate in Namibia according to the Namibia Revenue Agency's general tax brochure (NamRA, General Tax Information, read 2026-11-07). The same brochure says some supplies are taxed at 0% or exempt; this demo does not model that, so treat the VAT line as an illustration.
  • CLS thresholds. Layout shift below 0.1 is generally treated as good and above 0.25 as poor, following the Core Web Vitals guidance published at web.dev.
  • Connection speeds. The four profiles in table B are assumptions chosen to show the range, not measurements.
  • What was and was not tested. The measurements were checked in Chrome. Firefox, Safari, real phones and real slow networks were not tested, and the demo does not claim results for them.
  • Photographs. The photos were resized and cropped, which counts as a modification under the licences below. CC BY-SA photographs stay under their own licence; this does not change the licence of the rest of the site. Photos of people were not used.
